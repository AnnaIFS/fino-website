AI that fits the work you actually do

Three things, in this order. The people who will use it, the work they actually do, and then the tools. Most of what we build lives inside the workflows your team already runs, and we are at the keyboard with them the whole way.

The problem underneath

The tools are the easy part

The team that already hoards information now hoards it faster. The team that avoids hard conversations now has help writing the polite version. The team that trusts each other brings what they tried to the next meeting and gets faster together.

The same holds for the work itself. A process nobody has looked at in five years does not improve when it runs faster. It gets worse in less time, and it gets harder to see.

The tool is the same in every one of those. The team and the work are what differ.

Why we do this

It started inside our own work

Anna spent over a decade running operations inside fast-moving organizations before co-founding FINO. She knows what it looks like when capable people are buried under tools they did not choose.

When we started working with teams on Self-leadership, the same thing kept coming up afterwards. The inner work was shifting things. Then Monday came and people were still drowning in tools nobody had time to learn, fears nobody had named, and the background hum of falling behind.

Some people were already deep in it. Some were resisting without saying so. Most were in the middle, too busy to try and unsure where to start.

So we joined the two together. The Self-leadership practice we already do with teams, and the operations work itself. Workflows, SOPs and the internal tools a team runs on, built with the people who use them.

What we start with

The people and the work, before the tools

Before a tool goes near the work, we sit with the people who will use it.

The part that fears being replaced. The part that is excited and racing ahead. The part that has been overworking for years and is exhausted at the idea of one more thing to learn. The part protecting a workflow they built and secretly know is becoming obsolete. The part that stopped believing leadership would see this through.

Every fear in your team is doing a job. We start by listening to it. Then the rollout meets a team that is not fighting itself while it learns.

We look at the work the same way. How a job actually moves, where it stalls, which steps exist because of something that stopped being true years ago. If the process is what is broken, we say so. Putting a tool on top of it only gets you there faster.

What you have at the end

Your team owns all of it

01

A team that can meet AI together

Every team has a skeptic, an enthusiast, and a middle group too busy to try. We name what each is carrying and build shared language for it. People stop performing okayness and start using the tools in the open.

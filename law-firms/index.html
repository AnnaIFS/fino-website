What we do for firms

You already carry it. We help you carry it.

Every hour in a firm is billable or it is overhead, and improving how the work runs is neither. So it lands on you, on top of everything else. We bring an operations partner whose whole job is how the work runs. Anna has run operations for over ten years. You decide, we build it with your team, and it stays yours.

The calendar

One live calendar the office reads and updates, on the same link every time. Mediation dates get one link too. Everyone marks what works, you confirm, and it locks.

Intake and matter status

We set up one way for new matters to come in, so the details are entered once. Where each matter stands moves out of one person's head and into something the whole team reads and updates. Nobody emails for status, and nothing walks out of the door with the person.

Templates

One place holds the current version of each document, with who approved it and when, because that is what people check before they use one. New drafts start from the case information. Your team reviews and sends.

Case management: choosing and moving

Often we leave the system alone and build around it. When it does have to change, we run that decision with you. We research the vendors, run the demos, test billing before anyone signs, negotiate the terms, and lead the firm side of the migration.

It is the largest technology decision a firm makes. It is usually made once every ten years or so, by people carrying full caseloads.

Time and billing

Six minutes a day, per timekeeper, is more than four thousand dollars a year at a $175 rate. It goes when time is written on paper and retyped later, and when a hundred emails a day have to be read, saved and then billed. We redesign the flow so time gets captured where the work happens.

AI policy and safe AI use

Your attorneys may already be using AI. Better to know. Written rules come first, so nothing that names a client goes into a tool without a rule for it. Then tools your staff can use on real work. Then training, at their pace, in plain language.

The inner side

Software decisions in firms fail for people reasons.

Somebody is afraid of the new system, somebody is protecting the old one, and the change dies in a partners' meeting. That part is what we are trained for. It decides whether the software was worth buying.

  • The staff who have been here longest. Their version usually exists already, somewhere on somebody's desktop. We make it the standard, with their name on it.
  • The people who resist anything new. They are usually right to. So we start with two people who want it, and the time those two get back does the convincing.
  • You, carrying it all. We take tasks off your desk, and what you know gets written down, so the firm runs smoothly on the day you are out.

How we work

Questions

The concerns we hear most.

We have already hired for this.

Then you already know the work did not speed up. More people move more work only when the work has somewhere to go. We start by finding where it stops, which is usually two or three places, and none of them is a person.

My staff are already stretched.

That is the reason to do it, and it shapes how we do it. We start with short conversations with the people who do the work. Then it is short working sessions, a few times a week, on live matters. It replaces work they are already doing.

We handle confidential matters.

We work inside your existing permissions and confidentiality obligations, and we agree what we can and cannot touch before anything starts.

Our attorneys are already using AI and I am not sure what to do about it.

You are not unusual. We write the usage policy with you, set up the tools your staff can use on real work, and train them at their pace.

Do you sell the software?

No. We recommend vendors, and we take nothing from them. You get our read on where a product is weak as well as where it is strong.

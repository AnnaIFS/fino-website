What it is

Built around your team's own week

Most teams have the talent. What breaks down is what happens inside people when the stakes rise.

Someone shuts down. Feedback lands wrong. A hard conversation gets avoided for weeks.

Circles give a team a way to catch those moments while they are still happening, and to find the way back to clarity. Parts work is the map we teach from. The material is whatever your team is carrying.

Then the language spreads. People start using it in meetings, in feedback, in their one to ones. That is when things shift.

What it sounds like

A shared language, out loud

Teams tell us this is the part they did not expect. Sentences like these start appearing in ordinary meetings.

A part of me is getting defensive about that feedback. Give me a second.

I can feel my perfectionist part taking over on this. Let me reset.

I am activated right now. Thirty seconds before we continue.

We are all running hot after that call. Let us pause before the next decision.

The format

How a circle runs

Each session runs sixty to ninety minutes. Teaching, live practice, and reflection, with examples pulled from your team's own week. Sometimes breathwork, when the group needs to land before going deeper.

Sessions are participative and built around what is in the room that day. People work on real situations, live in the session, not case studies.

The fit

Is this right for your team?

A good fit when

  • A leadership team is navigating high stakes decisions or tension between people
  • Cross-functional teams need to collaborate under pressure
  • The organization is going through change, growth or restructuring
  • The talent is strong and the communication keeps breaking down

Not the right time when

  • You want a one-time talk or a quick fix
  • The team is not ready to look at what is going on underneath
  • What you need is traditional skills training

One way in

The Self-leadership Intensive

Half a day with your team. Eight weeks is a serious commitment, and this is how most teams find out whether they want it.

Usually online. In person when it makes sense.

We start with the idea that everyone is in two meetings at once. The one on the calendar, and the one running inside them. People work out what they do when the pressure comes. Then they say it to each other, which for most of them is a first.

It is not heavy. People laugh, and people also say things they have not said before.

Your team leaves with language they can use on Monday. You leave knowing whether you want the eight weeks.
